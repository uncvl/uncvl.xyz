  The Uncivilization World Building Project is an attempt to look for and examine ways and methods of collective production based on prespecified conceptual elements.

  ## Uncivilization and the World Building Project

  ### Organizational and Conceptual Entity

  We consider Uncivilization as a conceptual entity as well as an organizational one. While the **organizational entity** refers to the sum of people involved and how they function together, the **conceptual entity** refers to refined ideas these people create.

  ### Purpose of Uncivilization

  Primarily, Uncivilization focuses on **creation and operation methods** that we can apply in production and action phases in real-life practices.

  Further, Uncivilization undertakes **research and production activity** on anything related to the concept and themes of Uncivilization.

  ### World Building Project

  The Uncivilization World Building Project is meant to be a platform where we merge these two efforts.

  The main question is: can we utilise some methods to build a system by which we can **collaboratively develop a fictional world?**

  ## The Main Concept

  ### Notions

  The main conceptualization and ideation in the project are based on two pairs of notions:

  - **existence – extinction**
  - **civilization – uncivilization**

  ### Beginning and End of Civilization

  Together, these notions indicate certain points in **time and place**.

  When and where the civilization came into existence is our starting point. Putting ourselves at that point, we try to understand how and why it happened.

  Furthermore, we speculate about when and how the civilization will cease to exist.

  ### Extinction Events

  We investigate the impact of **mass extinctions** (extinction events) on the origination and collapse of civilization.

  Overall, we question the roles of **nature and humans** in these crucial turning points.

  ### Fields of Interest

  These topics we dwell on provide us with a **wide range of subjects** to explore.

  Considering the beginning of civilization, we can delve into anthropology, archaeology, sociology, linguistics, and so on.

  When we think of the end of civilization, we need to consider many subjects related to the environment and technology.

  > How comes the world to be here at all instead of the nonentity which might be imagined in its place? … from nothing to being there is no logical bridge.
  >
  > — William James, “On Some Hegelisms”, *Mind*, 1882

  ## Conceptual and Thematic Elements

  ### Function of the Concept

  **Concept** serves as the foundation upon which the project is built.

  It will act as a guiding principle that will shape the **overall direction**. With the use of concept, we will be able to give a particular common **message** all together. The concept, in essence, will provide a common **purpose**.

  ### Reuse of the Concept

  The main concept is specific, but it will evolve and transform over time as we proceed.

  We will reinterpret the main concept and adapt it to suit different contexts and themes.

  ### Difference between the Concept and a Theme

  It is important to understand the difference between the concept and a theme in the Uncivilization World.

  Concept determines the **overall direction**. Themes determine the **areas of research**.

  While the concept focuses on the **underlying idea or framework** of the project, a theme suggests a certain **topic or subject** to work on.

  ### Relation between the Concept and Themes

  Themes are going to provide us unifying nodes that connect different **elements** and **components** within the work.

  Concept is going to hold these nodes together, arrange and connect them to create a meaningful **whole**.

  ### Utilization of the Concept and Themes

  The concept is specific and focused. Themes, on the other hand, are varied, and they allow multiple interpretations and perspectives.

  Themes are where participants can engage actively and bring out their **creativity and knowledge**.

  The concept will provide participants with a ground for **criticism and review**.

  ## Contexts, Limitations and Curation

  ### Integrity of the Fictional World

  Throughout the overall process, an effort is going to be made to maintain a **sense of reality** that is perceived by all participants and observers.

  We must solve **contradictions**, fix **logical errors**, avoid **paradoxes**, and so on.

  This is achievable only by limiting ourselves to certain contexts, and these contexts need to be curated.

  ### Narrative

  Conceptual elements, as key factors, are used to channelize the attention to an area of context to build the **overall narrative**.

  Meanwhile, thematic elements are used to focus the attention on a specific area of context to build a **part of the narrative**.

  ### Constraints and Limitations

  Both elements, conceptual or thematic, will set constraints and limitations to direct the participant's focus toward a **common background** or a **specific area**.

  We will use these limited contexts to construct the basic idea, to propose a starting point, and for the further development of the fictional world.

  ### Supervising and Curation

  The concept is already determined by the founder of the project.

  Themes will be determined by the administrators of the project.

  Submissions to the project are going to be validated or discarded by an editorial team.

  ### The Board

  A board will be formed to discuss and decide the direction and expansion of the project.

  The board and the people they recruit will be responsible for organizing, managing, and maintaining the project.
