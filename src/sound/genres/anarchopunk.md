description_en: |-
  Anarcho-punk is subgenre of punk rock that promotes anarchism. Anarcho-punk sought to distance itself from the bands that have become too glossy and commercial and seek to hold punk to its roots by largely disregarding the verse/chorus structures that mainstream punk had displayed, playing with a purposeful rough sloppiness, allowing the shouted vocals that railed against societal and governmental injustices to become the primary 'instrument' on display.

  Often played as loud and fast as the band's skills allowed, the intent was to create sounds that were just as extreme as the message they tried to convey. Anarcho-punk showed that punk politics were more substantive and thoughtful than many believe. Topics related to anarchism like feminism, animal rights and environmental justice are common lyrical themes.

  Anarcho-punk bands often lived the anarchist lifestyle they extolled by living in squats or communes, participating in direct actions and practicing "do-it-yourself" (DIY) ethic.

  Anarcho-punk bands are often less focused on particular musical delivery and more focused on a totalized aesthetic that encompasses the entire creative process, from album and concert art, to political message, and to the lifestyles of the band members. According to the punk aesthetic, one can express oneself and produce moving and serious works with limited means and technical ability.

  Anarcho-punk has been one of the main musical, aesthetic and ideological influences for many punk subgenres including crust punk and D-beat.
