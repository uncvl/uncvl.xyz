description_en: |-
  Hardcore punk is a derivation of punk rock and it is generally faster, harder, and more aggressive than other forms of punk rock, while retaining punk's anti-establishment image and DIY ethics. The impact of powerful volume is important in hardcore. Hardcore emphasized speed and rhythmic intensity using unpredictable song forms and abrupt tempo changes. The songwriting has more emphasis on rhythm rather than melody. Other common characteristics include an extensive use of yelling and screaming, a stripped-down style of production and the prevalence of short-length songs.

  In hardcore, guitarists frequently play fast power chords with a heavily distorted and amplified tone. Guitar parts can sometimes be complex, technically versatile, and rhythmically challenging. Hardcore bassists use varied rhythms in their basslines, ranging from longer held notes (whole notes and half notes) to quarter notes, to rapid eighth note or sixteenth note runs. Some bassists play fuzz bass by overdriving their bass tone. Hardcore drumming, with the drummer hitting them aggressively, has been called the "engine" and most essential element of the genre's aggressive sound. Two other key elements for hardcore drummers are playing tight with the other musicians, especially the bassist. Some hardcore punk drummers play fast D-beat one moment and then drop tempo into elaborate musical breakdowns in the next. Hardcore vocalists often shout, scream or chant along with the music, using vocal intensity and an abrasive tone. Hardcore vocal lines are often based on minor scales and songs may include shouted background vocals from the other band members. Hardcore punk generally disavows commercialism, the established music industry and often addresses social and political topics with confrontational, politically-charged lyrics.

  The genre would eventually splinter into other subgenres and derivative forms in a similar manner as punk rock did. The interest of several hardcore punk musicians in various metal genres played an important role in the creation of various new styles, exemplified by crust punk, grindcore, metalcore.
